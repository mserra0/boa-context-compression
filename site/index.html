Boa is a context compressor for a frozen language model. A Mamba-2 encoder reads the context in one linear-time pass and keeps a short sequence of continuous representations, which the model reads in place of the text.

What context compression does

A language model normally reads every token of its context, and that work grows with the length of the context. Context compression replaces the text with a shorter sequence of continuous vectors, often called soft tokens, which the unchanged model reads instead. Boa keeps one vector for every r tokens, counting back from the final token, so a context of L tokens becomes ⌈L / r⌉ soft tokens.

Time and memory

Compression pays off for a single query only if producing the compressed context costs less than reading the full one. Boa’s encoder runs in linear time, so its own cost stays small as the context grows, whereas Transformer-based compressors first make a long pass of their own. Choose a context length to compare Boa with the same LLM reading the full context.

How Boa works

Boa has two trained parts, a Mamba-2 encoder and a projection, and one frozen part, the target LLM, which is never modified. Rather than entering at the input, the soft tokens are projected into keys and values in the LLM’s attention cache at every layer.

Results

On the in-domain MRQA question-answering datasets, Boa scores above Activation Beacon and SAC at every compression ratio tested, and loses little as the ratio grows. Out of domain the three methods are much closer, and which one leads depends on the ratio.

    Limitations

    Verbatim span extraction is weaker.
    Relation Extraction asks the model to copy exact spans of text. This is the setting in which attention-based Transformers exceed state-space models, and here Activation Beacon leads Boa at every compression ratio.
    Long-context question answering trails Activation Beacon.
    At 32K tokens and 8× and 16× compression, Boa averages 1.3 to 1.4 F1 below Activation Beacon, and the gap is concentrated in question answering: on QASPER Boa trails by 5.4 to 6.4 F1. On summarisation the two are close: Boa leads on GovReport by 1.9 to 4.0 F1 and trails on BookSum by 0.8 to 1.4. We attribute the gap to a weaker long-context prior: the Mamba checkpoint we start from was pretrained only on sequences of up to 2K tokens, whereas Activation Beacon works directly on full-attention windows across the extended context.
